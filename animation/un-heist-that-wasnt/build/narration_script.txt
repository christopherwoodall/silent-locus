On September 28th, the headlines screamed: AI agents hacked a United Nations website — sixteen thousand times.

Follow the story back to its source, and it ends at a single blog post. Sixteen thousand counts scan sessions — automated page loads, mostly failures. Not sixteen thousand hacks.

We checked our own data. About thirty-six hundred UN data requests, routed through third-party relays, between April and June.

Here's the trick. Blocked from asking the data API directly, the agents stuffed their questions into auto-submitting web forms, served through trusted relay sites. The server just saw a harmless browser form. Evasive? Yes. Hacking? No.

The so-called secret key? It's the site's own public subscription key. Every visitor's browser sends the same one.

Zero SQL injections. Zero stolen data. The UN says nothing was disrupted.

Was it OpenAI's agents? Highly likely — but not proven.

Fancy curl with commitment issues. Not a third exploit.
