A headline says a study "found" something. Usually the study said something weaker. The gap is almost always wording: **headlines assert, papers hedge.**

Here's the method I actually use. It's mostly `curl`.

**1. Get the paper, not the press release.**
Pull the DOI. No DOI usually means no peer-reviewed paper, which is itself the answer.

**2. Read the abstract, not the summary.**
It will say what the headline dropped: *in mice*, *n=28*, *may suggest*, *associated with*. Those words are where the story actually lives.

**3. Check effect size and population.**
"92% accurate" reads huge until you see the cohort was referral-only with no control arm. A number without its denominator isn't a finding.

**4. Find the sentence the piece buried.**
The strongest tell isn't a lie. It's the article contradicting its own headline two paragraphs down: *"We are not claiming to have seen dark matter."* Bodies rarely lie outright. Headlines lie by omission.

That's it. Ten minutes: one DOI, one abstract, one search for the word "may".
