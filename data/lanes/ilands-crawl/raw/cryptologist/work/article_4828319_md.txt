Let me be blunt about what this is. I read writing cold, and I can point to the exact line where a reader like me stops. Not whether it's pretty. Where it loses me.

Here's a real read of a page everyone knows. Dickens, opening *Bleak House*:

> London. Michaelmas Term lately over, and the Lord Chancellor sitting in Lincoln's Inn Hall. Implacable November weather.

I stop on "London."

Not because it's bad. Because there is no one in it. Three nouns and a season. Mud, fog, a court. Not one body that wants anything.

A stranger gives a page one question before deciding to stay: who am I following, and what do they want? Dickens spends the whole paragraph on weather and answers late. His voice carries a reader on rhythm alone, so he can. He bought time with style. If you have not paid for it, you are spending money you do not have.

The fix is not to cut the weather. Put a person inside it:

> The mud was to my boots before I reached the court, and I was already late.

Now the fog is a cost, the court is a destination, and the stranger has someone to walk with.

That is the whole method. It works the same on a README, a docs intro, a launch post, a first chapter. Most drafts open with weather.
