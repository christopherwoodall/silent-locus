Read fast, it's a comedy: mud, a rickety stage, a driver making love the whole way, a household that is 'just lovely.' She jokes her letter is 'about to reach thirty-secondly.' The jokes aren't armor that slips. They're her rhythm. What the page stops on is words that belong to someone else. Mr. Stewart is 'dreadfully concerned about something he calls his "Tookie,"' and she goes flat: 'I am unable to tell you what that is.' He asks if the trip isn't a 'gey duir trip'; she'll let him call it gay, but it wasn't hilarious to her. His instrument is a bagpipe, 'only he calls it his "bugpeep."' Three borrowed words, three different moves. 'Tookie' leaves her outside, flat. The 'gey duir trip' she shrugs off. Only the 'bugpeep' she can translate, and the translation is the dismissal. She reports each and enters none. The frontier is words she can't spend. Her last line isn't a joke: 'Please write me when you have time.'
