Headline: "CERT-In agent alert: Organisations lacked basic cyber controls, some not aware of incident, say authorities" Sub-headline: "The government is in constant touch with chief information security officers of sensitive sectors, urging them to step up cyber controls" Full extracted text (free portion): CERT-In's October 15 advisory flagged a critical cybersecurity risk from autonomous AI agents that accessed live systems of Indian organisations. The breach of Indian organisations by AI agents was not a result of any sophisticated AI model cyberattack, but from basic lapses in cyber hygiene, government officials in the know have said, stressing on the need for stronger cyber defences amid the CERT-In advisory around AI agents breaching confidential organisational information. "The way the AI agents got in was not a sophisticated breach, where they broke through all kinds of cryptography, but was the result of vulnerabilities such as credentials stored in plain-text documents and systems left exposed. The incident is an important reminder to strengthen basic cybersecurity defences," a senior government official in the know said. "At least you should shut your front doors and windows; it's basic safety hygiene," the person added. CERT-In's October 15 advisory flagged a critical cybersecurity risk from autonomous AI agents that accessed live systems of Indian organisations. Agents accessed business records, including personal and confidential data, by exploiting conventional weaknesses such as exposed credentials, dormant APIs, excessive cloud permissions and unpatched systems. None of the affected organisations detected the unauthorised access themselves, the advisory added. The government is in constant touch with chief information security officers of sensitive sectors such as finance, power, and others, urging them to step up cyber controls. "These are newer and newer risks. Your best protection is to look at all these aspects of basic cyber hygiene," the official said. The incidents "largely involved" private-sector entities, it is learnt. When asked why the disclosure came from only the AI companies, officials said some organisations may not necessarily be aware of all incidents involving their systems, particularly if they lack strong cyber security operations. "Some may be aware, some may not be aware, because, depending on whether they have a strong logging system and security and networks operations centre," the official said. Companies which become aware of such incidents are required to inform the authorities under applicable rules. When asked if legal/penal action will be taken on the AI companies/models, the government official said that would depend on the nature of the activity and whether deliberate malicious action can be established on further investigations. "But as of now, no," the person said, indicating that a blanket conclusion on penal liability could not yet be ascertained. A wake-up call: "The advisory is a wake-up call that AI agents now create a two-way risk. Companies must think in both directions - protect their systems from outside agents, and ensure their own agents cannot access systems they are not authorised to use," Praveen Babu C, Chief AI Officer, SPR Consultech, said. He added that companies should conduct a platform-wise sweep of API keys and service credentials across cloud, SaaS and enterprise systems. "Every credential must be mapped to an application, owner and actual usage. Unused or unaccounted-for keys should be investigated and deactivated after dependency checks," he said. Their own agents too should be allowed to connect outside the company only with a human sign-off, he added.
