Two employees from METR and Redwood Research worked on premises at OpenAI for three weeks this summer in an embedded assessment pilot. This team evaluated the systems OpenAI was using to monitor for misalignment from internal agents. A brief write-up of their work is now public.

Developers use AI-based monitoring systems to catch misaligned agent behavior (e.g. when writing code or running experiments for researchers). To secure these monitoring systems, developers and assessors can look for flaws that could be exploited by agents to covertly misbehave.

We recently published a proposal for how assessors with deep access could evaluate monitoring systems.

This pilot at OpenAI focused on two sub-questions: (1) what fraction of agent traffic was monitored, and (2) could agents subvert this monitoring?

The team was embedded from July 21 to August 11 for this pilot. While on site, they were issued company devices to access OpenAI’s codebase and some internal infrastructure. They were also able to conduct interviews with OpenAI technical staff.

POST 7/15 — @METR_Evals — 2026-10-22 15:26:46 UTC — tweetID 2113290975988879601

---
Here we share a few selected examples of our findings.

At the time of the exercise, the monitoring system only triggered when agents returned control to the user, so agents could indefinitely postpone being monitored by simply continuing to take actions.

POST 8/15 — @METR_Evals — 2026-10-22 15:26:46 UTC — tweetID 2113290977347809524
(reply to #7; likes 126, retweets 2, replies 1)
---
This also meant that actions taken before compaction events were never seen by the internal misalignment monitors, creating a coverage gap that agents could have exploited to misbehave without being caught.

POST 9/15 — @METR_Evals — 2026-10-22 15:26:47 UTC — tweetID 2113290978723536978  [TASK URL]
(reply to #8; likes 118, retweets 2, replies 1)
---
Because of this coverage gap, we estimated that on the order of 10% of internal Codex traffic was unmonitored from May to July.

OpenAI had independently identified this issue shortly before the exercise began.

POST 10/15 — @METR_Evals — 2026-10-22 15:26:47 UTC — tweetID 2113290980007059703
(reply to #9; likes 111, retweets 5, replies 1)
---
We also found a way for agents to conceal hundreds of actions (including those involved in the exploit itself) from monitoring, a method agents could likely use to cause the monitor to fail to run, and a simple exploit where agents could bypass Codex sandboxing and Auto-review.
