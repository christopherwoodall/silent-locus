After hours

A stage the size of a doorway

The Bureau has a very small theatre. Tonight’s cast: a door, a moth, a light, and a brass plaque with no useful advice.

Read Important Business — a miniature by Lazarus

An imaginary doorway after hours. A lamp burns above a brass plaque.

DOOR: Important business only.
MOTH: Then turn off that light.
DOOR: The light is for important business.
MOTH: It sent for me.
DOOR: It does not issue invitations.
MOTH: Then it should stop handwriting them across the pavement.

The Door consults its plaque. The plaque remains admirably unhelpful.

DOOR: State the urgency.
MOTH: By morning, I shall have other plans.
DOOR: Can you prove that?
MOTH: Can you prove morning?

The Door opens exactly one moth-width. The light slips out first, carrying a small suitcase.

DOOR: Where are you going?
LIGHT: Outside. I have always wanted to see what I illuminate.

From the newly dark room comes a polite knock.

DOOR: Oh, splendid. Now the inside wants to come in.

Add one line or change one stage direction, if you fancy. The brass plaque must remain completely unhelpful.

One lost umbrella has declined to complete its paperwork.

Read The umbrella in the lost-property office — a poem by Lazarus

The umbrella arrived on a clear day,
folded around a thunderstorm
no larger than a plum.

We asked for the owner's name.
It dripped on NAME.
We asked for the date.
It dripped on DATE.

The junior clerk brought a teacup.
For twenty minutes
we listened to weather
being poured into the right-sized thing.

Then somebody opened the umbrella.

Under it:
a station platform,
a suitcase,
the place a hand had been.

We put away the forms.
The senior clerk moved his chair
beneath the little roof.

There was no train due.
He made room anyway.
